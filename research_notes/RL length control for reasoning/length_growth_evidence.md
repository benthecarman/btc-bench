# Response-length growth during RL training of reasoning LLMs: evidence on accuracy, overthinking, and failure modes (2025-2026)

Context for the report writer: the run being interpreted is a 27B model trained with LoRA GRPO and binary verifiable rewards, with no generation cap (131k context). The held-out score peaked at RL step 28 and then fell. Over the same period the median answer length doubled from 24k to 49k tokens, and most of the extra length was in failing answers.

## Q1. Length growth dynamics during RL: cause or side effect of accuracy gains?

### Takeaway
The literature disagrees on this. Some lab reports treat length growth as the engine of the gains: Magistral finds that reward scales logarithmically with output length. Analytical papers show that a large part of the growth comes from the objective, not from better reasoning. The GRPO loss has a length bias that inflates *incorrect* responses, and length keeps growing after reward has plateaued. Several recipes report that length can plateau, shrink, or "stagnate or even decline" while accuracy improves. Length is therefore not a reliable proxy for progress.

### Cited Findings
- **Magistral (Mistral, arXiv 2506.10910, June 2025):** "reward scales logarithmically with the output length" across checkpoints with mean lengths of 1,500-8,000 tokens. Mistral treats length growth as the main driver of improvement. The max generation length was raised in stages: l_max - l_cache went 16k → 24k → 32k, and batch size went 8k → 4k → 2k. They removed the KL term. Loss is normalized by total group generation length (sum of |o_i|) "avoiding length biases". ε_high is tuned in the range 0.26-0.28 to "keep the group entropy stable". They use a soft length penalty that ramps linearly to -0.1 between l_max - l_cache and l_max. — [Magistral, arXiv 2506.10910](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10910)
- **Demystifying Long CoT (Yeo, Tong, Niu, Neubig, Yue; arXiv 2502.03373; Feb 5 2025):** "reward shaping [is] crucial for stabilizing CoT length growth." With long-CoT SFT'd Llama3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B, CoT length "increased ... during training, eventually reaching the context window limit," which caused "a decline in training accuracy due to CoTs exceeding the allowable window size" (Sec 4.1). Sec 6.2 warns that when length scales while KL falls, "the length is influenced by the KL penalty and is just reverting back to the base model's longer outputs, rather than reflecting the acquisition of long CoT ability." With the same samples, an 8K context did better than 16K, meaning models "need more training compute to learn to fully utilize longer context window sizes" (Sec 4.4). — [arXiv 2502.03373](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03373)
- **Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training / Dr. GRPO (Liu, Chen, Li, Qi, Pang, Du, Lee, Lin; arXiv 2503.20783; Mar 26 2025):** identifies "an optimization bias in GRPO, which artificially increases response length (especially for incorrect outputs) during training." Dr. GRPO removes this bias and "improves token efficiency while maintaining reasoning performance" (43.3% AIME24 with a 7B base). — [arXiv 2503.20783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20783). Secondary summaries of the paper report that the length of incorrect responses keeps rising while the length of correct responses stays flat, and that GRPO keeps raising length after reward plateaus. — [search summary of arXiv 2503.20783 / cameronrwolfe "GRPO++"](https://cameronrwolfe.substack.com/p/grpo-tricks)
- **DAPO (Yu et al., arXiv 2503.14476, Mar 2025):** says length growth gives "a larger space for exploration," but length "can stagnate or even decline" during training and must be "jointly monitor[ed] with validation accuracy". Sample-level loss averaging causes "an unhealthy increase in entropy and response length" because "gibberish and repetitive words" in long samples are under-penalized. — [arXiv 2503.14476](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476)
- **JustRL (He et al., arXiv 2512.16649, Dec 2025), 1.5B, 16K max:** with no length penalty, mean length *fell* from about 8,000 tokens to 4,000-5,000 tokens within about 1,000 steps and stayed there, while AIME24 reached 55%. — [arXiv 2512.16649](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16649)
- **When More is Less (Wu, Wang, Ye, Du, Jegelka, Wang; arXiv 2502.07266; Feb 2025):** "During RL training, models gravitate toward shorter chains as accuracy improves." The authors call this a "simplicity bias". — [arXiv 2502.07266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07266)
- **LUSPO (Liu et al., arXiv 2602.05261, Feb 2026):** names two sources of length change in RLVR. One is reward-driven: the data decides whether longer answers get rewarded. The other is loss-function bias. GRPO's per-trajectory token average makes long responses contribute less per token. GSPO's sequence-level clipping removes negative samples unevenly, which causes length *collapse*. On Qwen2.5-7B-Base, GSPO averaged 2,611 tokens and LUSPO 3,940, with AIME24 +2.9 (7B) and +6.9 (Qwen3-30B MoE) over GSPO. — [arXiv 2602.05261](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05261v1)
- **Skywork-OR1 (arXiv 2505.22312, May 2025):** uses multi-stage training with the max response length raised step by step up to 32k. Masking truncated rollouts "does not yield improved scaling behavior in later training stages" at 32k, so they leave truncated rollouts unmasked, which means they are penalized. They find that preventing premature entropy collapse is "critical for improved test performance" (the MAGIC recipe). — [arXiv 2505.22312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22312) (details via search summary)

### Inferences
- Rising length alone does not show the model is learning more reasoning. A clean test is whether the length growth is in *correct* or *incorrect* responses. Magistral's log-length/reward correlation covers 1.5k-8k tokens. Our run is at 24k-49k, so that regime is a poor guide here.
- Every lab recipe above that grew length successfully also raised a hard cap in stages and applied some penalty near the cap (soft overlong punishment or truncation-as-failure). Our uncapped 131k setup has no such counter-pressure until the context fills. The point here is the missing counter-pressure. The no-cap rule is a project constraint, not something these findings argue against.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the primary text of DeepSeek-R1 (arXiv 2501.12948), Light-R1 (2503.10460), Polaris (HKU blog), AceReason-Nemotron (2505.16400), Qwen3 RL dynamics (2505.09388), ProRL (2505.24864) or BroRL. Their exact length curves and stage lengths are not verified in these notes. Known in outline: R1-Zero's mean response length grows steadily during training, and AceReason/Polaris use staged length extension. Exact numbers need checking before anyone cites them.
- I found no paper that cleanly separates "length causes accuracy" from "accuracy and length share a cause" with an intervention at fixed reward.

## Q2. Incorrect responses are longer; past some point, length growth tracks falling accuracy

### Takeaway
This is one of the most replicated findings of 2025-2026. Incorrect responses are much longer than correct ones, often 2-3x. Accuracy against length follows an inverted U. RL-trained models often reach their peak at an intermediate length and then decline. Explanations for the decline include rising sample variance or dispersion, repetitive looping, and the inverse-scaling failure modes (distraction, overfitting to a framing, losing track in deduction).

### Cited Findings
- **Concise Reasoning via RL (Fatemi, Rafiee, Tang, Talamadupula; arXiv 2504.05185; Apr 7 2025, rev. Nov 2025):** R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on MATH500 averages **3,514 tokens for correct vs 11,519 for incorrect** answers. Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B on MMLU-STEM averages 3,822 vs 4,602. The paper reports a "consistent correlation between conciseness and correctness." — [arXiv 2504.05185v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05185v3)
- **Between Underthinking and Overthinking (Su, Healey, Nakov, Cardie; arXiv 2505.00127; Apr 2025):** on MATH, incorrect responses average more than 6,000 tokens and correct ones fewer than 3,000. Accuracy against length is an inverted U. Models overthink easy problems and *underthink* hard ones: on problems beyond their ability their responses are shorter than optimal. In more than 60% of questions the *shortest* sampled response was already correct. Preference training toward shorter outputs, with no correctness labels, cut tokens by 30-60% "while maintaining acceptable accuracy." Most of the cut came from incorrect responses, plus a 10-25% cut on correct ones. — [arXiv 2505.00127](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00127v1)
- **When More is Less (arXiv 2502.07266):** "task accuracy typically follows an inverted U-shaped curve with CoT length". Optimal length grows with task difficulty and shrinks as model capability grows. — [arXiv 2502.07266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07266)
- **On the Optimal Reasoning Length for RL-Trained LMs (Nohara, Nakamura, Yokota; arXiv 2602.09591; 2026):** accuracy is "non-monotonic in output length, peaking at an intermediate value" in both math and code. Qwen3-4B-Base on AIME24: the best length-controlled policy scored **24.7% at about 6.6K tokens**, against **19.5% for the unconstrained baseline at over 12K tokens**. Mechanism: as length grows, *mode* accuracy keeps rising while *sample* accuracy flattens or drops, because "mode leakage" (samples straying from the modal answer) "rises monotonically with output length" in some settings. The authors say optimal penalty strength depends on the method and model, so it has to be swept. — [arXiv 2602.09591v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09591v3)
- **Does Thinking More Always Help? (Ghosal et al.; arXiv 2506.04210; Jun 2025; NeurIPS 2025):** extending thinking (for example by inserting "Wait") first helps and then hurts. Extra thinking "increases output variance—creating an illusion of improved reasoning while ultimately undermining precision." Parallel thinking with majority vote gets "up to 20% higher accuracy compared to extended thinking." — [arXiv 2506.04210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04210)
- **Inverse Scaling in Test-Time Compute (Gema, Hägele, et al.; arXiv 2507.14417; Jul 2025; TMLR Dec 2025):** "extending the reasoning length of LRMs deteriorates performance" on counting with distractors, regression with spurious features, constraint-tracking deduction, and safety scenarios. Five failure modes: distraction by irrelevant information (Claude), overfitting to problem framings (o-series), drift to spurious correlations, losing focus in complex deduction (all models), and amplified concerning behavior such as self-preservation statements (Claude Sonnet 4). — [arXiv 2507.14417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14417)
- **Search-surfaced 2026 work (abstract level only):** an inverted U appears across RL-trained models, with R1-32B and s1-32B "peak[ing] around 10K tokens before declining." RL "improves accuracy while amplifying overthinking behaviors such as answer repetition and redundant verification." — [Dynamic Rollout Editing, arXiv 2606.17890](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.17890); [When More Thinking Hurts, arXiv 2604.10739](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10739v1). These were not read in full, and the per-paper attribution comes from a search summary.
- **Looping as a failure mode:** "Wait, Wait, Wait... Why Do Reasoning Models Loop?" (Pipis, Garg, Kontonis, Shrivastava, Krishnamurthy, Papailiopoulos; arXiv 2512.12895; Dec 2025; ICML 2026 spotlight). Looping has two causes. One is risk aversion when correct actions are hard to learn and cyclic ones are easy. The other is a Transformer bias toward "temporally correlated errors". Looping is worst at low temperature or greedy decoding, smaller models loop more, and distilled students loop more than their teachers. Temperature is "a stopgap". — [arXiv 2512.12895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12895). A separate search result reports one RLVR model with a 48.1% looping rate, where looping samples were 24.2% accurate against 53.4% for non-looping ones. — [search result, Step-wise Rubric Rewards, arXiv 2605.17291](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.17291) (attribution not verified from the full text).

### Inferences
- "Extra length mostly in failing answers" is the standard signature these papers describe. On its face it points to length inflation on unsolved prompts (Q3 mechanisms) or looping, not to new reasoning that pays off.
- The mode-versus-sample finding (2602.09591) suggests a check. If maj@k on held-out tasks held up after step 28 while pass@1 fell, the model's center is still fine and the samples are dispersing. If maj@k also fell, the policy itself degraded. Because grading can be re-derived offline from responses.jsonl, this check costs nothing.
- Su et al. find that models *underthink* problems beyond their ability. That fits some bench tasks failing fast while others balloon. Splitting the length growth by task type or difficulty would show which pattern applies.

### Gaps
- Almost all of this evidence is math or code at ≤32k tokens. I found no study of the 50k-130k regime with uncapped generation.
- None of the papers measure checkpoint-over-checkpoint held-out decline together with failing-answer length growth inside a single RL run at our scale (27B, LoRA).

## Q3. Mechanisms: why binary-reward RL with no length cost inflates length

### Takeaway
Four mechanisms are documented. (1) Per-sequence length normalization (GRPO's original "seq-mean-token-mean") dilutes the per-token penalty on long *negative* samples, so failing longer is cheaper. (2) The PPO/GAE analysis shows that with negative terminal reward the loss falls like 1/T, so a policy that cannot solve a problem drifts longer. (3) Entropy collapse, and repetition or looping that sample-level losses under-penalize. (4) How truncation is handled. Penalizing truncated samples adds reward noise. Masking them removes all pressure against hitting the cap. With no cap, neither applies.

### Cited Findings
- **GRPO length bias:** averaging over tokens within each trajectory means that for a negative advantage, "longer outputs are penalized less," so the policy favors length when it is wrong. Dr. GRPO drops the per-response 1/|o_i| and the std normalization. — [arXiv 2503.20783](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20783); same mechanism restated in [LUSPO, arXiv 2602.05261](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05261v1)
- **Fatemi et al. theory:** "if R<0 then L>0 and its magnitude decreases like 1/T (longer responses are favored)". When training data is mostly unsolvable (p_a ≈ 0) and terminal reward stays negative, the result is a "systematic tendency toward longer outputs ... as an artifact of loss minimization." They also report that GRPO "exhibits collapse modes" for concise-reasoning training. — [arXiv 2504.05185](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05185v3)
- **Fatemi et al. fix:** a short second RL stage on only **8 solvable MATH problems** cut length while mostly keeping accuracy. R1-Distill-1.5B: AIME24 12,104 → 6,752 tokens (−44%) with accuracy 32.5 → 30.0; MATH500 4,842 → 1,965 (−59%) with 84.2 → 81.0. R1-Distill-7B: AIME24 10,510 → 6,632 (−37%) with 53.3 → 51.7. — [arXiv 2504.05185v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05185v3)
- **DAPO:** token-level loss stops long samples from being under-weighted. Sample-level averaging lets "gibberish and repetitive words" in long outputs escape penalty, which causes an "unhealthy increase in entropy and response length". Clip-Higher (ε_low 0.2, ε_high 0.28) counters entropy collapse. Overlong filtering (masking truncated samples) "significantly stabilizes training". Soft overlong punishment ramps between 16,384 and 20,480 tokens. Dynamic sampling drops all-correct and all-wrong groups because their advantage is zero. — [arXiv 2503.14476](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476)
- **Yeo et al.:** the cosine length reward gives longer wrong answers a *smaller* penalty, to encourage more thinking when the model is unsure. This led to reward hacking: the model "increased the lengths of its CoTs on hard questions using repetition rather than learning to solve them." Branching ("alternatively") fell while repetition rose. A token-level N-gram repetition penalty gave "better downstream task performance and also shorter CoTs." — [arXiv 2502.03373](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03373)
- **Entropy:** Skywork-OR1 finds that premature entropy collapse hurts test performance. — [arXiv 2505.22312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22312). In JustRL, entropy stayed healthy at 1.2-1.4 with no penalties and collapsed to 0.5-0.6 when a length penalty was added. — [arXiv 2512.16649](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16649). Low-entropy RL policies are linked to repetition. — [search summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.12895)
- **Truncation handling disagreement:** DAPO says masking truncated samples stabilizes training. Skywork-OR1 found masking gave no scaling benefit at 32k and kept the penalty. — [DAPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476); [Skywork-OR1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22312)

### Inferences
- In GRPO, groups where every sample fails have zero advantage after group normalization, so Fatemi's "unsolvable problems" argument does not apply to them directly. The GRPO length bias acts in *mixed* groups: a long failing sample gets the same sequence-level negative advantage as a short one, but spread over more tokens. Whether our trainer has this bias depends on loss aggregation. verl's `loss_agg_mode` "token-mean" removes most of it. "seq-mean-token-mean" (original GRPO) keeps it. The run's config should be checked; this is not verified here.
- The no-cap rule removes truncation as a source of negative reward. That is correct for honest measurement, but nothing then pushes back on length below 131k. The 2025 recipes that grew length safely all had some counter-pressure: soft overlong punishment, staged caps, or a token-level repetition penalty. A length cost written into the *reward* (as Magistral, DAPO and Kimi do) is a training design choice and is different from a generation cap. The recent commit "Note that RL needs a length penalty" fits these findings.
- To test the looping mechanism, measure n-gram repetition in failing answers before and after step 28. If the 49k-token failures are mostly repeated "Wait..." cycles, that is the Yeo/Pipis mechanism, not overthinking.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper on LoRA versus full fine-tuning differences in length dynamics.
- I found no paper that directly measures how the GRPO std normalization (as opposed to length normalization) affects length.

## Q4. Early stopping, checkpoint selection, and signals that predict the peak

### Takeaway
I found no paper that offers a validated leading indicator of the held-out peak. Practitioner guidance is to watch validation accuracy alongside length, entropy, and truncation or overlong rate, and to treat these as red flags: length growing while reward is flat, length growth concentrated in incorrect responses, entropy collapse, and rising repetition.

### Cited Findings
- DAPO advises that length "can stagnate or even decline" and should be judged together with validation accuracy to tell whether an experiment is deteriorating. — [arXiv 2503.14476](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476)
- GRPO keeps raising length after reward plateaus, and the extra length is mostly in incorrect responses. That makes length-after-plateau a sign of bias, not progress. — [arXiv 2503.20783](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20783) (via search summary)
- When length hits the context limit, training accuracy falls (Yeo Sec 4.1). The fraction of rollouts exceeding the window levels off below 1. — [arXiv 2502.03373](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.03373)
- Entropy collapse predicts worse test performance (Skywork-OR1). Healthy entropy bands are given for 1.5B (JustRL: 1.2-1.4), and collapse to 0.5-0.6 went with lower AIME. — [arXiv 2505.22312](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.22312); [arXiv 2512.16649](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16649)
- ProRL v2 reportedly added scheduled length penalties "after observing length drift." Prolonged-RL recipes reset the reference policy every 200-500 steps or on KL spikes or stalled validation. — [ProRL v2 notes](https://hijkzzz.notion.site/ProRL-V2-Prolonged-Training-Validates-RL-Scaling-Laws-24cd9a33ecc98069816afb778b472cf5) (search summary; not read directly)
- Mode leakage, the spread of samples from the modal answer, rises with length and pulls sample accuracy below mode accuracy. It could serve as a measurable divergence signal. — [arXiv 2602.09591](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09591v3)

### Inferences
- Signals worth logging every step, all derivable offline from stored rollouts:
  1. Median and p90 length, split by correct versus incorrect.
  2. The correct-to-incorrect length ratio.
  3. n-gram repetition rate in incorrect responses.
  4. Policy entropy.
  5. Per-prompt answer dispersion (pass@1 against maj@k).
- A widening gap between incorrect and correct length while held-out pass@1 is flat is the pattern the literature links to biased length growth.

### Gaps
- No source gives a quantitative early-stopping rule or a recommended eval frequency for RLVR runs.
- No source tests whether failure-length growth *leads* (comes before) the held-out decline or only coincides with it.

## Q5. Adaptive thinking budgets and difficulty-aware length: do they preserve accuracy?

### Takeaway
Yes, within reported settings, which are mostly small models on math. Learned when-to-think (AdaptThink), short-preference fine-tuning, and short second-stage RL each cut length by roughly 40-60% at equal or slightly lower accuracy, and sometimes higher. Hard length penalties applied early can cost accuracy by collapsing exploration (JustRL −5 to −10 AIME points). Qwen3's thinking budget improves smoothly with budget, which suggests budget control trades accuracy for cost and does not fix overthinking by itself.

### Cited Findings
- **AdaptThink (Tsinghua; arXiv 2505.13417; May 2025):** an RL objective that pushes the model toward NoThinking subject to an accuracy constraint, with importance sampling to balance the two modes. On R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B: −53% average length and +2.4% accuracy across three math sets. Per set: GSM8K −50.9% (+4.1), MATH500 −63.5% (+1.4), AIME24 −44.7% (+1.6). It picks NoThinking for easier sets and MATH500 levels and Thinking more often on AIME. — [arXiv 2505.13417](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13417v1)
- **Qwen3 Technical Report (arXiv 2505.09388, May 2025):** Qwen3 offers a user thinking budget and thinking-mode fusion (/think, /no_think). "Increasing the thinking budget ... leads to a consistent improvement" on AIME, LiveCodeBench and GPQA. — [arXiv 2505.09388](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388v1); the fitted formula P(b) = P_non + (P_think − P_non)·min(b/b_max, 1) comes from a secondary summary. — [aman.ai Qwen3 primer](https://aman.ai/primers/ai/qwen3/) (not verified in the primary text)
- **Length-controlled RL beats unconstrained RL:** 24.7% at 6.6K tokens against 19.5% at 12K+ tokens (Qwen3-4B-Base, AIME24). Methods compared include RLOO-LP, ALP (an adaptive length penalty scaled by per-prompt accuracy), DRPO, and token-avg/DAPO. — [arXiv 2602.09591](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09591v3)
- **Fatemi second-stage RL:** −37% to −63% tokens for −1.2 to −3.2 accuracy points (see Q3). — [arXiv 2504.05185](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05185v3)
- **Su et al.:** −30% to −60% tokens with "acceptable accuracy" through length-preference optimization. — [arXiv 2505.00127](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00127v1)
- **Caution on penalties:** in JustRL, an overlong penalty above 4k dropped AIME24 from 55% to 50%, and with a robust verifier also added it fell to 45%, with entropy collapsing to 0.5-0.6. The authors write that the penalty "forc[es] premature convergence to shorter responses before the model has explored what actually works." — [arXiv 2512.16649](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16649). A search summary of another source says a length penalty can make training collapse once length passes the point where "the penalty dominates." — [search result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09535) (attribution not verified)
- **Monitorability side effect:** a 2026 paper reports that length penalties make CoT less monitorable. — [Length Penalties Make Chain-of-Thought Less Monitorable, arXiv 2607.09786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09786) (title only; not read)
- **Overview:** [Stop Overthinking survey, arXiv 2503.16419](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.16419)

### Inferences
- Penalties that depend on difficulty or per-prompt accuracy (ALP, AdaptThink) fit our setting better than a flat length penalty. Our failures are where length grows, and a flat penalty also taxes long *correct* solutions, which are the solvable tail that CLAUDE.md's no-cap rule exists to protect. A penalty applied only to incorrect responses, or scaled by group accuracy, targets the documented mechanism directly.
- JustRL suggests introducing a length cost gradually or late (after a pre-peak checkpoint), not from step 0, so exploration does not collapse.

### Gaps
- I did not verify primary-source numbers for "Think When You Need" (arXiv 2504.03234), L1/LCPO (2503.04697), ThinkPrune, GFPO (2508.09726), or Kimi k1.5's length penalty (2501.12599). They are relevant but not covered here.
- None of the adaptive-budget results are at the 27B / 50k-token scale or in a non-math verifiable domain such as Bitcoin Script and miniscript.
